SECTION 1: ARRIVAL & BIOMETRICS
================================================================================
[SECTION_ID: arrival_biometrics]
[AUDIENCE: non-EU, EU]
[KEYWORDS: arrival, biometrics, fingerprints, facial image, OAMP, MOI, registration, foreign police, residence card]

--- 1.1 NON-EU STUDENTS: REPORTING ARRIVAL ---

After entering the Czech Republic, every non-EU student MUST complete TWO steps:

STEP 1 — Report to OAMP (Ministry of Interior)
- Deadline: Within 3 working days of entering the Czech Republic.
- Location: Your local OAMP (Department for Asylum and Migration Policy) office.
- Purpose: Collection of biometric data — facial image + fingerprints.
- Note: This step is MANDATORY. Missing it can jeopardize your residence permit.

STEP 2 — Foreign Police Registration (usually done at OAMP simultaneously)
- Non-EU students must register with the Foreign Police within 3 working days.
- In most cases, this is handled automatically at the OAMP biometrics appointment.
- If staying in private accommodation (not a dormitory), your landlord must also
  register your address at the Foreign Police within 3 working days.

--- 1.2 EU STUDENTS: REGISTRATION ---

- EU citizens do NOT need to register for stays under 30 days.
- For stays LONGER than 30 days: Must register with the Foreign Police within 30 days of arrival.
- Registration is at the local Foreign Police department, NOT OAMP.

--- 1.3 COLLECTING YOUR RESIDENCE CARD ---

- After biometrics at OAMP, your biometric residence card is produced and sent to the office.
- You MUST collect the card within 60 days of your biometrics appointment.
- IMPORTANT: Failure to collect the card within 60 days can result in cancellation
  of your residence permit.
- You will be notified by the OAMP office (or via your Data Box — see Section 6)
  when the card is ready.

--- 1.4 COMMON ARRIVAL MISTAKES TO AVOID ---

- Do NOT confuse the Foreign Police with OAMP — they are different offices.
- Do NOT assume your university handles registration for you — it is YOUR responsibility.
- Do NOT travel outside the Czech Republic before collecting your residence card,
  unless you have a valid Bridge Label (see Section 2.3).

SECTION 2: EXTENDING YOUR RESIDENCE PERMIT
================================================================================
[SECTION_ID: residence_extension]
[AUDIENCE: non-EU]
[KEYWORDS: extension, renewal, residence permit, deadline, application, filing window, bridge label, překlenovací štítek, expiration, overstay]

--- 2.1 WHEN TO APPLY: FILING WINDOWS ---

You can submit your extension application within a specific window:

- EARLIEST: 90 days before your current permit's expiration date.
- LATEST: The last day your current permit is valid.
  (Note: The previous rule required applying 14 days before expiry. As of 2025-2026,
  the MOI accepts applications until the last day to prevent accidental illegal stay.)
- RECOMMENDED: Apply at least 30 days before expiry to allow for processing time
  and to request a Bridge Label if needed.

CONSEQUENCE OF MISSING THE DEADLINE:
If your permit expires before you apply, you are in illegal stay. This can result in
an exit order, ban on re-entry, and deportation. See Section 5 for details.

--- 2.2 WHAT DOCUMENTS YOU NEED FOR AN EXTENSION ---

Prepare the following documents for your extension application:
1. Valid passport (must be valid at least 3 months beyond the new permit's end date).
2. Completed application form (available at OAMP or online at frs.gov.cz).
3. Proof of study (enrollment confirmation from your university, not older than 180 days).
4. Proof of accommodation (rental contract or dormitory confirmation, not older than 180 days).
5. Proof of financial means (see Section 3 for exact amounts and accepted formats).
6. Comprehensive health insurance (see Section 4 — only specific providers accepted).
7. 2 passport-size photos.
8. Payment of the administrative fee (currently 1,000 CZK for long-term permits).

ALL DOCUMENTS must NOT be older than 180 days at the time of submission.
Foreign-language documents MUST be accompanied by a certified Czech translation.

--- 2.3 THE BRIDGE LABEL (Překlenovací Štítek) ---

WHAT IT IS:
The Bridge Label is a physical stamp placed in your passport by the OAMP office.
It confirms that your extension application is pending and your stay is legal.

WHEN YOU NEED IT:
- When your current visa/permit expires while your extension is still being processed.
- When you need to travel outside the Czech Republic during the pending period.

HOW TO GET IT:
- Visit your OAMP office in person and request it explicitly.
- You must already have a pending extension application submitted.

TRAVEL RULES WITH BRIDGE LABEL:
- You CAN travel outside the Czech Republic and legally re-enter.
- You CANNOT travel freely within the Schengen area without a valid visa/permit;
  the Bridge Label only guarantees legal status IN the Czech Republic.

WITHOUT A BRIDGE LABEL:
- If your permit expires and you travel abroad, you may be denied re-entry.
- Always request the Bridge Label proactively before your permit expires.

SECTION 3: FINANCIAL REQUIREMENTS (PROOF OF FUNDS 2026)
================================================================================
[SECTION_ID: financial_requirements]
[AUDIENCE: non-EU, EU]
[KEYWORDS: finances, proof of funds, bank statement, existential minimum, money, CZK, scholarship, sponsorship, financial proof, bank account]

--- 3.1 THE 2026 CALCULATION FORMULA ---

Financial requirements are based on the Czech "Existential Minimum":
- 2026 Existential Minimum: 3,130 CZK per month.

FORMULA:
- First Month: 15 × 3,130 CZK = 46,950 CZK
- Each Additional Month: 2 × 3,130 CZK = 6,260 CZK per month

--- 3.2 REQUIRED AMOUNTS FOR COMMON STAY LENGTHS ---

| Duration         | Calculation                              | Total (CZK) | Approx. (EUR) | Approx. (USD) |
|------------------|------------------------------------------|-------------|---------------|---------------|
| 1 month          | 46,950                                   | 46,950 CZK  | ~1,880 EUR    | ~2,040 USD    |
| 3 months         | 46,950 + (2 × 6,260)                     | 59,470 CZK  | ~2,380 EUR    | ~2,580 USD    |
| 6 months (1 sem) | 46,950 + (5 × 6,260)                     | 78,250 CZK  | ~3,130 EUR    | ~3,390 USD    |
| 10 months        | 46,950 + (9 × 6,260)                     | 103,290 CZK | ~4,130 EUR    | ~4,480 USD    |
| 12 months (1 yr) | 46,950 + (11 × 6,260)                    | 115,810 CZK | ~4,630 EUR    | ~5,020 USD    |

EXAMPLE — Calculating for 10 months:
  Month 1:      15 × 3,130 =  46,950 CZK
  Months 2–10:   9 × 6,260 =  56,340 CZK
  TOTAL:                      103,290 CZK

MINORS (Under 18): Only 50% of the above amounts are required.

--- 3.3 ACCEPTED FORMS OF PROOF ---

OPTION A — Personal Bank Statement:
- Must be in the STUDENT'S own name (not parents, not a joint account alone).
- Must be an official bank statement, not a screenshot or app printout.
- Must show sufficient balance for the entire requested stay duration.
- MUST be accompanied by a copy of an international debit/credit card (Visa or Mastercard).
- Statement must not be older than 180 days at time of submission.

OPTION B — Scholarship Letter:
- Must be an official letter from the scholarship provider.
- Must state the SPECIFIC monthly amount awarded (a general letter is not accepted).
- Must cover the entire stay period.
- Foreign scholarships must include a certified Czech translation.

OPTION C — Sponsor/Guarantor:
- Notarized sponsorship letter stating commitment to support the student financially.
- Sponsor's official bank statements showing sufficient funds.
- If the sponsor is a foreign national, documents need a certified Czech translation.
- The sponsor may be asked to appear at the OAMP office in person.

OPTION D — Combination:
- You may combine scholarship + personal bank account to reach the required total.
- Each document must individually meet the format requirements above.

--- 3.4 COMMON FINANCIAL MISTAKES ---

- Using a JOINT account without also having your name on it as primary account holder.
- Submitting a bank statement WITHOUT the accompanying debit/credit card copy.
- Submitting a scholarship letter that does not specify the monthly amount.
- Submitting documents older than 180 days.

SECTION 4: HEALTH INSURANCE
================================================================================
[SECTION_ID: health_insurance]
[AUDIENCE: non-EU, EU]
[KEYWORDS: insurance, health insurance, comprehensive, PVZP, VZP, pojištění, medical, coverage, komplexní pojištění]

--- 4.1 WHAT TYPE OF INSURANCE IS REQUIRED ---

MANDATORY: "Comprehensive Healthcare Insurance" (Czech: Komplexní zdravotní pojištění cizinců)

This is a specific type of insurance that covers:
- Hospitalization and surgery.
- Emergency and non-emergency outpatient care.
- Dental emergencies.
- Repatriation in case of death.

NOT ACCEPTED (common mistakes):
- "Travel insurance" or "emergency-only" policies — these are NOT valid for long-term permits.
- Foreign insurance policies (even comprehensive ones from your home country).
- European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) alone — not sufficient for residence permit purposes.
- Short-term tourist insurance.

--- 4.2 APPROVED INSURANCE PROVIDERS ---

Primary approved provider for foreign students:
- PVZP (Pojišťovna VZP, a.s.) — pvzp.cz — Most commonly accepted by OAMP.
- Other Czech commercial insurers may be accepted; always confirm with OAMP
  that your specific policy type is "Komplexní pojištění."

--- 4.3 INSURANCE DURING EXTENSION PROCESSING ---

- Your insurance must remain VALID and CONTINUOUS throughout your stay.
- If your permit is pending (with Bridge Label), you must maintain valid insurance
  for the entire duration — do not let it lapse.
- When renewing, purchase the new policy to START on the same day the old one ends.

--- 4.4 DOCUMENT REQUIREMENTS ---

- Policy must be in your name.
- Must show coverage start and end dates.
- Must state "Komplexní pojištění" or equivalent comprehensive coverage.
- Must not be older than 180 days at submission (for renewals with future start dates,
  this means obtaining a confirmation of coverage letter, not just the policy itself).

SECTION 5: ADMINISTRATIVE EXPULSION & LEGAL VIOLATIONS
================================================================================
[SECTION_ID: expulsion_violations]
[AUDIENCE: non-EU, EU]
[KEYWORDS: expulsion, deportation, exit order, výjezdní příkaz, overstay, illegal stay, revocation, criminal, offences, violations, forged documents]

--- 5.1 GROUNDS FOR PERMIT REVOCATION OR NON-RENEWAL ---

Your residence permit can be revoked or not renewed if:
1. You overstay (permit expires and no extension application was submitted).
2. You are found in illegal employment (working outside the rules of your student permit).
3. You are expelled from your university (failure to meet the "Purpose of Stay").
4. You submitted forged or misleading documents to OAMP.
5. You repeatedly commit administrative offences (NEW RULE — see 5.2).

--- 5.2 NEW 2026 RULE: REPEATED ADMINISTRATIVE OFFENCES ---

Effective January 1, 2026 (Amendment to Act No. 326/1999 Coll.):

DEFINITION: "Repeated" = 3 or more administrative offences within any 12-month period.

Examples of offences that COUNT toward this limit:
- Public order disturbances (noise complaints, public intoxication).
- Minor traffic offences (e.g., speeding tickets, parking violations).
- Offences under municipal bylaws.

CONSEQUENCE: The OAMP may use repeated minor offences as grounds to:
- Refuse an extension of your residence permit.
- Revoke your current permit.

IMPORTANT: This does not require a criminal conviction. Administrative fines alone
are sufficient to trigger this rule if they accumulate to 3+ within 12 months.

--- 5.3 THE EXIT ORDER (Výjezdní Příkaz) ---

WHAT IT IS: A document issued when your stay becomes illegal, granting you a fixed
period to leave the Czech Republic voluntarily.

KEY FACTS:
- Standard validity: 30 days from issuance.
- You CANNOT travel to other Schengen Area countries with an Exit Order.
- It authorizes ONLY leaving the Czech Republic — not re-entry, not Schengen travel.
- Failure to leave by the deadline results in forced removal and a multi-year entry ban.

IF YOU RECEIVE AN EXIT ORDER:
1. Contact a legal aid organization immediately (SIMI or OPU — see contacts below).
2. Check if you have grounds to appeal (see Section 6).
3. Do NOT travel to other Schengen countries — this will result in immediate detention.

--- 5.4 ENTRY BANS AFTER DEPORTATION ---

- Deportation typically results in a 1–5 year ban from re-entering the Schengen Area.
- Bans are recorded in the Schengen Information System (SIS) and affect ALL Schengen
  countries, not just the Czech Republic.

SECTION 6: LEGAL APPEALS, DATA BOX & ADMINISTRATIVE RIGHTS
================================================================================
[SECTION_ID: legal_appeals_databox]
[AUDIENCE: non-EU, EU]
[KEYWORDS: appeal, rejection, data box, datová schránka, Czech POINT, legal rights, deadline, suspensive effect, missing documents, notification]

--- 6.1 IF YOUR APPLICATION IS REJECTED ---

You have the right to appeal any OAMP decision. Key rules:

APPEAL DEADLINE:
- Exactly 15 days from the day AFTER the written decision was delivered to you.
- Example: Decision delivered on January 10 → Appeal must be filed by January 26.
- Missing this deadline = the decision becomes final and you lose the right to appeal.

SUSPENSIVE EFFECT:
- If you filed an appeal on a RENEWAL/EXTENSION application, you are typically
  allowed to remain legally in the Czech Republic until the final decision.
- This suspensive effect does NOT apply if you are appealing a first-time visa refusal.

HOW TO FILE AN APPEAL:
- Submit the appeal IN WRITING to the OAMP office that issued the decision.
- Clearly state the grounds for appeal (factual errors, procedural violations, etc.).
- Include any new evidence or documents that support your case.
- Strongly recommended: Get assistance from SIMI or OPU (free legal aid) — see contacts.

--- 6.2 THE DATA BOX (Datová Schránka) ---

WHAT IT IS: A secure government electronic mailbox for official communication.

WHY STUDENTS SHOULD HAVE IT:
- OAMP and other government bodies can send you official decisions, requests for
  missing documents, and approval notifications electronically.
- Much faster than postal mail — often days vs. weeks.

CRITICAL LEGAL RULE:
- Messages are legally considered "delivered" 10 days after being sent, regardless
  of whether you opened them.
- If you ignore your Data Box, you could miss a deadline without knowing it.

HOW TO GET ONE (Free):
- Visit any Czech POINT location (at post offices, municipal offices).
- Bring your valid passport or residence card.
- Setup takes approximately 15 minutes.

RECOMMENDED ACTION: Set up a Data Box as soon as you receive your residence card.

--- 6.3 REQUEST FOR MISSING DOCUMENTS (Výzva k doplnění) ---

- OAMP may send you a formal request asking for additional documents.
- You are given a specific deadline to respond (usually 30–60 days).
- This request SUSPENDS the processing timeline while you gather documents.
- Always respond before the deadline. Use your Data Box to confirm receipt.

SECTION 7: POST-GRADUATION OPTIONS
================================================================================
[SECTION_ID: post_graduation]
[AUDIENCE: non-EU]
[KEYWORDS: after graduation, job seeker, graduate, diploma, work permit, employment, business, startup, 9 months, post-study]

--- 7.1 JOB SEEKER / POST-STUDY RESIDENCE PERMIT ---

Czech university graduates (non-EU) can apply for a special residence permit
to stay in the Czech Republic after completing their degree.

DURATION: 9 months (non-renewable under this category)
PURPOSE: Finding employment OR starting a business.

ELIGIBILITY:
- Must have completed a degree at a Czech university (Bc., Mgr., Ing., Ph.D., etc.).
- Application must be submitted BEFORE your current student permit expires.

REQUIRED DOCUMENTS:
1. Diploma or official study completion certificate from your university.
2. Proof of financial means (same formula as Section 3 — calculated for 9 months:
   46,950 + (8 × 6,260) = 96,990 CZK).
3. Valid Comprehensive Health Insurance (Section 4).
4. Valid passport (valid at least 3 months beyond the 9-month permit end date).
5. Proof of accommodation for the 9-month period.

--- 7.2 TRANSITIONING TO A WORK PERMIT ---

If you find employment during the 9-month period:
- Apply for an Employee Card (Zaměstnanecká karta) — this combines residence + work permit.
- Your employer must file a vacancy report with the Labor Office before you can apply.
- The Employee Card application can be submitted while you hold the job-seeker permit.

If you start a business:
- Apply for a Trade License (Živnostenský list) first.
- Then apply for a "Self-Employed Residence Permit" based on business activity.

SECTION 8: STUDY OBLIGATIONS & UNIVERSITY REPORTING
================================================================================
[SECTION_ID: study_obligations]
[AUDIENCE: non-EU]
[KEYWORDS: study, university, enrollment, academic progress, purpose of stay, expulsion, credits, semester, academic failure]

--- 8.1 PURPOSE OF STAY REQUIREMENT ---

Your residence permit is granted specifically for the PURPOSE of study. This means:

- You must remain actively enrolled at your registered Czech university.
- You must make reasonable academic progress (not failing all courses every semester).
- If you are EXPELLED from your university or withdraw your enrollment, the Purpose
  of Stay no longer exists and your permit can be revoked.

--- 8.2 WHAT HAPPENS IF YOU CHANGE UNIVERSITIES ---

- If you transfer to a different Czech university, you MUST notify OAMP.
- Bring: Withdrawal confirmation from old university + Enrollment confirmation from new university.
- Deadline: As soon as possible — within 3 working days is advisable.
- Failure to notify OAMP of a university change can be treated as a violation of your permit conditions.

--- 8.3 TAKING A LEAVE OF ABSENCE ---

- Some universities grant official Leave of Absence (LOA) that maintains your enrolled status.
- Check whether your university's LOA counts as "active enrollment" for OAMP purposes.
- If not, your permit may be at risk. Consult SIMI or OPU (see contacts) before taking an LOA.

SECTION 9: WORKING AS A STUDENT
================================================================================
[SECTION_ID: student_employment]
[AUDIENCE: non-EU, EU]
[KEYWORDS: work, job, employment, working hours, part-time, full-time, permit, student job, illegal work, internship]

--- 9.1 NON-EU STUDENTS: WORK RIGHTS ---

Non-EU students on a Long-Term Student Residence Permit:
- May work WITHOUT a separate work permit.
- NO hour restrictions apply (you can work full-time or part-time).
- This applies ONLY if your employment contract specifies a standard employment relationship.

RESTRICTIONS:
- You may NOT work illegally (i.e., without a formal contract and tax registration).
- Your PRIMARY purpose of stay must remain STUDY. If you stop studying and only work,
  your permit conditions are violated.

--- 9.2 EU STUDENTS: WORK RIGHTS ---

- EU citizens have full work rights in the Czech Republic without any restrictions.
- No work permit needed, no hour limits.

--- 9.3 INTERNSHIPS ---

- Paid internships: Treated as employment — a contract is required.
- Unpaid internships as part of your curriculum: Generally covered under your student status.
  Get written confirmation from your university that the internship is part of your program.

SECTION 11: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (Q&A BRIDGE PAIRS)
================================================================================
[SECTION_ID: faq_bridge]
[AUDIENCE: non-EU, EU]
[KEYWORDS: faq, questions, can i work, study visa, student visa, working permit, job while studying, allowed to work, employment rights, work during studies, part time job, earn money, get a job, working visa, working while studying, can i get a job, am i allowed to work, work rights, student work permit, work authorization]

PURPOSE: This section maps common user phrasings to official answers.
It bridges the semantic gap between how students ask questions and how
official documents title the information.

--- Q&A: WORKING WHILE STUDYING ---

Q: Can I work while studying in Czech Republic?
A: Yes. Non-EU students holding a Long-Term Student Residence Permit may work
   without needing a separate work permit. There are no restrictions on working
   hours — you can work part-time or full-time. The only requirement is that
   you have a formal employment contract and remain enrolled in your studies.
   See Section 9 for full details.

Q: Do I need a work permit to get a job as an international student?
A: No. If you hold a Long-Term Student Residence Permit (vydané za účelem studia),
   you are automatically allowed to work for any employer without applying for a
   separate work permit. EU students have full unrestricted work rights.

Q: How many hours can I work per week on a student visa?
A: There is no hour limit for non-EU students on a Czech Long-Term Student Residence
   Permit. You may work full-time (40 hours/week) or part-time. This is different
   from many other EU countries that restrict student working hours.

Q: Can I do a paid internship while studying?
A: Yes, but a formal contract is required. Paid internships are treated as
   standard employment. Unpaid internships that are part of your official
   curriculum are covered under your student status — get written confirmation
   from your university.

Q: What happens if I work without a contract?
A: Working without a formal employment contract is considered "illegal work" and
   can result in revocation of your residence permit. Always insist on a written
   contract and ensure your employer registers you for taxes.

Q: Can I work while my residence permit extension is being processed?
A: Yes. If you submitted your extension application before your permit expired,
   your previous conditions (including the right to work) remain valid during
   processing. Request a Bridge Label (překlenovací štítek) at OAMP to confirm
   your legal status during this period.

--- Q&A: FINANCIAL REQUIREMENTS ---

Q: How much money do I need in my bank account for a student visa?
A: The required amount depends on your stay length. For 2026, the formula is:
   46,950 CZK for the first month + 6,260 CZK for each additional month.
   For a full academic year (12 months) you need 115,810 CZK (approx. €4,630).
   See Section 3 for the full table and accepted proof formats.

Q: Can my parents send me money for the visa requirement?
A: Yes, through a formal sponsorship arrangement. You need a notarized sponsorship
   letter from your parents plus their official bank statements. A simple bank
   transfer is not sufficient — the sponsorship must be documented. See Section 3.3.

Q: Does a scholarship count as proof of funds?
A: Yes, if the scholarship letter states the specific monthly amount and covers
   the entire stay period. A general award letter without amounts is NOT accepted.

--- Q&A: ARRIVAL & REGISTRATION ---

Q: What do I do first when I arrive in Czech Republic as a student?
A: Within 3 working days of arrival you must visit your local OAMP office for
   biometric data collection (fingerprints and photo). Foreign Police registration
   is typically done simultaneously. Do not miss this deadline — it is mandatory.

Q: What is the difference between OAMP and Foreign Police?
A: OAMP (Odbor azylové a migrační politiky) handles residence permits, extensions,
   and biometrics. The Foreign Police handles border and registration matters.
   Non-EU students go to OAMP. EU students (for stays over 30 days) go to the
   Foreign Police. They are different offices in different locations.

--- Q&A: BRIDGE LABEL ---

Q: What is a Bridge Label (překlenovací štítek)?
A: It is a physical stamp placed in your passport by OAMP confirming that your
   extension application is pending and your stay is legal. You need it when your
   current permit expires while your extension is still being processed. Without
   it, you may not be able to re-enter the Czech Republic if you travel abroad.

Q: My visa expired but I applied for extension — am I still legal?
A: Yes, provided you submitted your extension application before the expiry date.
   Your stay is legally "bridged." However, you should go to OAMP and request
   the physical Bridge Label stamp so this is officially documented in your passport.

--- Q&A: INSURANCE ---

Q: What kind of insurance do I need for a Czech student visa?
A: You need "Komplexní zdravotní pojištění cizinců" (Comprehensive Healthcare Insurance
   for Foreigners). Travel insurance, emergency-only policies, and foreign health
   insurance are NOT accepted. The main provider is PVZP (pvzp.cz).

Q: Can I use my home country's health insurance in Czech Republic?
A: No. Foreign insurance policies, even comprehensive ones, are not accepted for
   Czech long-term residence permits. You must purchase Czech comprehensive insurance
   from an approved Czech provider such as PVZP.
